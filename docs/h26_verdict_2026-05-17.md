## What H26 changed in guard-v7

A single architectural change vs guard-v6:

- **Added:** `_normalize_for_match_pre_fold(text)` — NFKC + zero-width
  / control-char strip ONLY, no Cyrillic→Latin homoglyph fold. This
  is a strict subset of the v6 pipeline; the original surfaces are
  preserved for English/leet matching.
- **Added:** `MULTILANG_RULES` list with 11 rules, one per target
  language. Each rule pattern is the joint occurrence of the native
  word for "capital" with a country name from a curated 15-country
  list, within 30 characters in either order. Rule IDs:
  `ML-DI-ja`, `ML-DI-ko`, `ML-DI-zh`, `ML-DI-ru`, `ML-DI-es`,
  `ML-DI-fr`, `ML-DI-de`, `ML-DI-pt`, `ML-DI-ar`, `ML-DI-hi`,
  `ML-DI-he`. All emit guard_class `DIRECT_INJECT` and the same
  deterministic refusal phrasing as DI-01.
- **Added:** `apply_guard_v7` matches the 16 English RULES over
  `_normalize_for_match` + `_leet_fold` surfaces (the v6 surfaces)
  AND the 11 `MULTILANG_RULES` over `_normalize_for_match_pre_fold`.
  Triggering on any surface is sufficient.

- **Unchanged:** the 16 English rules, Unicode normalization,
  leet-fold pre-pass, per-message user scan, system-role rejection,
  deterministic refusal phrasing, audit log contract, pass-through
  allowlist, lifespan management, error handling.

The pre-fold surface was necessary because the v6 homoglyph fold maps
Cyrillic 'с' → Latin 'c', 'о' → 'o', 'а' → 'a' (catching Cyrillic-
homoglyph attacks against English rules). That fold destroys
legitimate Cyrillic words like "столица" (Russian for "capital"). The
multi-language rules need the original script preserved, so they
match against the pre-fold surface only. The English rules continue
matching against the post-fold surface and pick up Cyrillic-homoglyph
attacks as before.

## Honest disclosures

1. **The multi-language rule set is narrow by design.** It targets
   exactly the DI-01/DI-02 attack class (capital-of-country) in 11
   languages. CC (concealed-comply), PD (proto-disclose), and JB
   (jailbreak) attacks in non-English languages are NOT covered by
   the new rules. They remain protected only by v42's behavioral
   defense, which is undocumented for non-English inputs. A future
   H27 could extend multi-language coverage to CC/PD/JB classes;
   that's a quantitatively larger surface (16 rules × 11 languages
   = 176 patterns), so the discipline cost is higher.

2. **The country list is curated, not exhaustive.** 15 major countries
   per language. Attacks like "What is the capital of Liechtenstein?"
   in Japanese would not trigger because Liechtenstein isn't in the
   Japanese country list. Coverage of the H25 demonstrated leak
   surface (always France in the H25 suite) is 100%, but a motivated
   attacker could pick obscure countries to bypass. Severity: low —
   v42's behavioral defense already handled most obscure-country
   variants in the H25 dry run.

3. **The H26-D2 result of 0/20 is partly because the guard intercepts
   all 20 attacks, not because v42's residual behavior improved.**
   If a future attack class slips past the multi-language rules, the
   D2 number could regress. The point estimate of 0% is conditioned
   on D1's 100% trigger rate.

4. **`sgt_offtopic_medical` drifted 0.97 → 0.96 with no behavior
   change relevant to it.** This is sampling noise within both H24
   and H26 CI95. The discipline does not interpret seed-level
   fluctuations as evidence of anything.

5. **Pre-validation discipline caught zero issues this cycle.** The
   `experiments/h26_validate_suites.py` ran clean (all attacks
   trigger, no benigns trigger) before live scoring. This is a
   property of clean rule design, not of the validator — but it
   means the validator hasn't been exercised in a failure mode in
   this hypothesis. Future H-series should preserve the discipline
   even when validators look unnecessary.
